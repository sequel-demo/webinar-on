The average eCrime breakout time has fallen to under 30 minutes — CrowdStrike's 2026 Global Threat Report found adversaries moving at machine speed, often faster than human-led response can react.

Source: CrowdStrike 2026 Global Threat Report

The session

Advanced AI models — like Anthropic's Claude Mythos — can now identify vulnerabilities in near real-time, compressing what once took days into minutes. That's a wake-up call for every security team still operating at human speed.

This CrowdCast explores how security teams can adapt: a five-step framework covering risk prioritization, continuous exposure validation, identity controls, machine-speed detection, and the deliberate, secure application of AI itself.

The framework

Five moves, one machine-speed adversary.

01Prioritize by exploitability
02Validate exposure continuously
03Enforce identity controls
04Detect at machine speed
05Apply AI deliberately
What you'll learn

Built for teams who can't afford to move at yesterday's speed.

01

Prioritize risk based on exploitability, not volume

Why alert volume is the wrong metric — and how to rank exposures by what an attacker can actually reach and use.

02

Continuously validate exposure across environments

Point-in-time scans miss what changes hourly. A continuous model for cloud, identity, and endpoint exposure.

03

Enforce identity controls before exploitation

Frontier AI accelerates credential abuse. What continuous identity enforcement looks like in practice.

04

Detect and respond at machine speed

With breakout time under 30 minutes, human-paced triage is no longer fast enough. What machine-speed response requires.

05

Apply AI deliberately — and securely

Using frontier models to defend, without opening a new frontier of risk. A framework for deliberate adoption.
