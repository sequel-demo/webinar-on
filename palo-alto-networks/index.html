The briefing

Each year, thousands of organizations experience a cyber incident. This year’s Unit 42 report analyzed over 750 major incidents across every major industry in more than 50 countries, and the data shows a clear shift in how attacks unfold.

Threat actors are moving faster, increasingly leveraging identity and trusted connections, and expanding attacks across multiple attack surfaces. Identity weaknesses played a material role in nearly 90% of investigations — more often than not, attackers aren’t breaking in, they’re logging in. In 23% of incidents, attackers leveraged third-party SaaS applications, bypassing traditional perimeters entirely.

The accelerating speed, scale and complexity of these intrusions mean the window between initial access and business impact is shrinking. Most breaches, however, still succeed due to preventable gaps in visibility and security controls. This session walks through what changed, what it means for the controls you already own, and where defenders are still winning.

The compressed kill chain

Four stages. Seventy-two minutes.

AI is being used in reconnaissance, phishing, scripting and operational execution — which enables machine-like speed at scale. Here is what that collapse looks like in practice.

T + 0 min
Initial access
Valid credentials or tokens, not an exploit. Nothing trips a traditional perimeter control.
T + ~15 min
AI-assisted recon
Automated discovery of the identity estate, SaaS integrations and data stores.
T + ~40 min
Lateral movement
Privilege escalation across fragmented identity estates; 87% of intrusions span multiple attack surfaces.
T + 72 min
Exfiltration
Data leaves the environment before the first alert has been triaged.
87%of intrusions involved activity across multiple attack surfaces — endpoint, network, cloud, SaaS and identity at once.
