About the webinar

The cost of a connected device doesn't stop at assembly.

A Bill of Materials captures component and manufacturing cost. It does not capture the cost of making a device connect, stay secure, stay updated, and stay supported for the ten or more years it sits in the field.

Building cellular connectivity in-house adds certification, carrier management, provisioning, cloud infrastructure, and long-term maintenance to a hardware program. Buying a pre-certified connectivity module transfers most of that work to a vendor.

In this live session we'll walk through the six cost categories a Bill of Materials leaves out, put real numbers on each one, and share the criteria for deciding which path fits your product, from trailers and reefers to batteries, brakes and cargo doors.

This session will run less than an hour, and we'll send you the recording afterward, but attending live is always better!

The six lines missing from your BOM

Blues calls the complete view the New BOM. We'll take each line in turn, with the numbers behind it.

01

Provisioning

Configuring SIMs, network settings and cloud credentials in the field, and why it hits hardest when you retrofit vehicles already in service.

02

Security

Why security is a recurring cost rather than a project, and how cellular moves much of the network burden onto the carrier.

03

Cloud platform

The infrastructure every device needs behind it to route, store and act on its data for a decade in the field.

04

Testing & certification

FCC, ISED, CE/RED and PTCRB, charged per market and per radio, and how a pre-certified module changes the math.

05

Fulfillment & deployment

Duties, VAT and excise on cross-border shipments, which commonly add $30 to $40 to a $100 device entering a new market.

06

Employee time

The engineering hours spent on carrier management and maintenance instead of the product you're actually trying to differentiate.
