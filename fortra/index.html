The argument

In a world that runs on data, the hard part was never adopting AI. It was answering a much older question at a speed nobody had planned for: where does our sensitive data actually live, and who just handed it to a model?

Most security teams aren't stalling on ambition. They're stalling on trust. A classification scheme built for file shares and email doesn't survive contact with a workflow where an employee pastes a customer list into a chat window, an agent reads a document store it was never scoped to, and a vendor's copilot indexes a folder somebody shared in 2021 and forgot.

Meanwhile the attack side is compounding. 456% more genAI-enabled scams in a year means the volume and quality of pretexting both went up at once — and a 271% surge in data volume expected by 2030 means the surface you're defending grows faster than the team defending it.

So the question isn't whether to use AI in your security program. It's which side of the equation you point it at first. This session walks through the data-centric version: classify at the source, enforce at the boundary, and let the models do the part humans were never going to finish — finding the 80%.
